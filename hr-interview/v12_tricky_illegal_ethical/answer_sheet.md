## The pattern for a question you'd rather not answer

1. Stay calm. Don't accuse.
2. Find the worry behind the question (will you stay? will you be available? will you fit?).
3. Answer the worry, not the question.
4. Bring it back to the job.
5. If pushed: "I'd prefer not to discuss that." (And notice what this tells you about the workplace.)

---

## 1. "Are you married? Planning (more) children soon?"

**Structure:** keep family life private, politely; answer the worry (commitment, availability); back to the job.

**Example (returning after a break):** "I'd like to keep my family life private. What I can tell you is that I'm fully ready to return. My childcare is arranged, and I can meet the hours this role needs."

## 2. "What's your surname? Which community is that?" / "Do you fast? Will you need leave for festivals?"

**Structure:** don't discuss community or religion; ask to be judged on your work; offer your work instead. For leave: "within company policy".

**Example:** "I'd like to be judged on my work. Can I tell you about my last two years in sales?"
**Example (leave):** "I'll plan my leave like everyone, within company policy."

## 3. "How old are you, exactly? Won't you get bored in this role?"

**Structure:** don't apologise for your age; talk about what you bring; answer the worry (will you stay, will you fit).

**Example:** "I'd rather talk about what I bring. I've led teams through two big system changes, and I enjoy mentoring younger people. I'm looking for a role I'll stay in for years. This one fits."

## More tricky questions, with one-line tips

| Question | One-line tip |
|---|---|
| Will your husband let you travel? | "I'm comfortable with the travel this role needs." |
| What does your father do? | A short answer, then back to your own work |
| Any health issues? | Say whether you can do the job's duties; details are private |
| Do you drink? | "Not at work." Then move on |
| Where are you really from? | Name your city, then your experience |
| Are you pregnant, or planning to be? | "I'd like to keep that private. I'm committed to this role and its hours." |

## 4. "Would you lie for the company?"

**Structure:** a clear no; then show loyalty through honesty, with an example.

**Example:** "No. But I'd work hard to find an honest way to protect the company. If a delivery slips, I'd tell the client early, with a new plan."

## 5. "Our team will verify your dates and last CTC. Anything to tell us first?"

**Structure:** tell the truth early; give exact dates; explain any gap in one or two lines; offer documents.

**Example (a gap):** "Yes. I left my last job in March 2023. I spent two years caring for my father and retraining in automation. My last CTC is exactly as in my documents, and I'm happy to share them."

**What verification often checks (it differs by company):** dates of joining and leaving, designation, last CTC,
degrees and certificates, sometimes references and address. Keep relieving letters and payslips safe.

## 6. "To confirm your joining, pay a refundable training deposit."

**Structure:** don't pay; ask for official details; verify the company yourself (its real website, a phone number you find on your own).

**Example:** "Thank you. I don't pay fees to join a company. Could you share your official address and HR contact?"

**Offer letter red flags:**

- Any fee or deposit to join
- No company address, or an email from a free mail account
- A salary different from what you agreed
- A vague role, or a bond or service agreement you never heard about
- Pressure to sign today

If the company is real but something differs from what you agreed: ask politely, in writing, before you sign.

## 7. Accepting an offer well

**Structure:** read the whole letter (salary, role, joining date, bond, notice terms); ask about anything unclear in writing;
reply warmly within the time given. Many people resign from their current job only after the signed offer letter.

**Example:** "Thank you for the offer. I'm happy to accept, and I look forward to joining on the agreed date."

## 8. "So, are you joining us?" (when you're declining)

**Structure:** thank them; say you've accepted another role; keep the door open. A call first, then in writing. Never ghost.

**Example:** "Thank you for the offer, and for your time. I've decided to accept another role. It fits my goals a little more closely. I'd be glad to stay in touch."

## 9. Resigning: "Why are you leaving us? Is it the money?"

**Structure:** thank them for what you learned; the positive reason you're moving; a promise of a proper handover.
Resign in person or on a call first, then a short, polite letter. Before deciding on a counter-offer, ask whether
more money fixes the reason you started looking.

**Example:** "Sir, I've learned a lot here. I've accepted a role that helps me grow in testing. I'll finish my notice period properly and hand over everything."

**Last day:** a short goodbye email (thanks, personal contact if you like, positive). Collect your relieving letter and last payslips.

## Four takeaways

1. Answer the worry behind a personal question, then return to the job.
2. Never lie. Frame the truth well.
3. Any fee to join is a red flag.
4. Decline and resign with grace. People remember how you leave.

*Rules differ by place: check yours. Nothing here guarantees an outcome.*
