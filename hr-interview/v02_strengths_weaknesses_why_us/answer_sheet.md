# Crack the HR Interview: Strengths, Weaknesses, and Why Should We Hire You?

## Answer sheet

Write your own answer under each question. Keep every answer **true**: an example is a template, not a costume.
Say each answer aloud, and prepare for one follow-up question.

**What these questions check:** Do you know yourself? Can you prove it? Does it fit this job? Can you see your own gaps?

---

### 1. What are your strengths?

**Strong answer structure**
1. Pick two strengths that matter for this job.
2. Prove each one with a small, real story or habit.
3. Tie them to the role.

**Avoid:** a list of nice words (hardworking, sincere, dedicated, punctual) with no examples. Punctual is the minimum, not a strength.

**Example answer** (a bank clerk going for an officer post)
> My first strength is accuracy. I balance my cash to the last rupee, and I double-check every entry before I close the counter.
> My second strength is patience. Older customers ask for me when they need help with mobile banking.

### 2. What is your greatest weakness?

**Strong answer structure**
1. A real weakness that is **not** central to the job.
2. What you are doing about it.
3. A result so far (and honesty that it's still a work in progress).

**Avoid:** "I work too hard", "I'm a perfectionist" (sounds rehearsed or hiding). Never name a core skill of the job.

**Example answer**
> I get nervous speaking in front of big groups. In college I avoided presentations, so last year I joined a weekly speaking club
> at work. I've presented at four team meetings since then. I still get nervous, but I prepare notes and it goes fine.

### 3. Give me another weakness

**Strong answer structure**
- Same shape as above: smaller, true, with a habit you've built.

**Avoid:** panicking and saying something harmful for the role ("I don't really like mornings" for a job that starts at seven).

**Example answer**
> Sometimes I say yes to too many tasks. Now I check my list before I agree to new work.

### 4. How would you rate yourself in (a skill) on a scale of one to ten?

**Strong answer structure**
- A fair number.
- What you can do now (specific).
- What you are still learning.

**Avoid:** a very high number you can't defend under one hard question, or a low number that sells you short.

**Example answer**
> Seven. I build pivot tables and charts every week. I'm still learning macros.

### 5. Why should we hire you?

**Strong answer structure**
1. Read the job description: pick their top two needs.
2. For each need: say it in their words, then your proof (something you actually did).
3. Close with why here.
4. If you lack one need: say so honestly, show the closest thing you've done, and how you'd learn the rest.

**Avoid:** "I really need this job" or "I'm a fast learner": both about you, with no proof.

**Example answer** (an accountant applying to an audit firm)
> You need someone who can run audits independently: I've led three small audits end to end. You're also moving to new accounting
> software: I helped my current firm switch last year, and I trained the team. And I want to grow into a senior role here.
> That's why I applied.

### 7. Why do you want to work for our company?

**Strong answer structure**
1. Research first: website and recent news, the job post, reviews from employees; if you can, talk to someone who works there.
2. One or two specific facts about them (don't recite their website).
3. Link their plans to your experience.

**Avoid:** "It's a big, well-known company" and leading with pay (salary has its own place later in this course).

**Example answer** (a warehouse supervisor at a logistics company)
> I read that you're opening a new distribution centre next year. I helped set up two new shifts at my warehouse. I'd like to
> bring that to a growing site, and learn your routing systems.

### 8. Why this role?

**Strong answer structure**
- What you are running **towards**, not what you are running from.
- Real proof that you already enjoy part of this work.
- Never bad-mouth your current job.

**Example answer** (a teacher moving to instructional design)
> I've taught science for six years, and my favourite part is designing lessons. During the school closures I made video lessons
> for my students, and other teachers started using them too. This role lets me design learning for many more students, not just
> my own class.

## More questions like these (quick practice)

| Question | Quick tip | Your answer (one line) |
|---|---|---|
| What do you know about us? | Two facts from research, one link to you | |
| What are you working to improve? | A real skill, the current step | |
| Why are you a good fit for our culture? | One of their values you have lived | |
| What's your biggest strength for this role? | One strength, one proof | |
| Why should we not hire you? | Stay calm: a small gap and your plan, then stop | |
| What would you do in your first month? | Listen, learn, one small win | |

---

## Four takeaways
1. Strengths: two that fit the job, each with proof.
2. Weakness: real, not core, and what you're doing about it.
3. Why hire you: their need, then your proof.
4. Why us, why this role: do the research, and show a direction.

*General interview guidance, not a guarantee of any outcome. Practices differ by country, company and role.*
