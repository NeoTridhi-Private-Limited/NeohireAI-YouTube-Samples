# Crack the HR Interview: Stress, Pressure and Curveballs (answer sheet)

Interviewers don't ask whether you feel stress. Everyone does. They ask whether you stay useful when it gets hard.
Never just claim "I work well under pressure": show your method and one true example. Keep each answer to about one minute.

**The pattern for every pressure question**
1. Your method: what you actually do when it gets busy (sort, plan, warn early).
2. One true example where the method worked (STAR: situation, task, action, result).
3. A small human habit (a breath between calls, asking for help) that makes it believable.

**If your mind goes blank**
- Breathe out slowly.
- Pause: to the interviewer, silence looks like thinking.
- Ask: "May I take a moment to think about that?"
- Repeat the question in your own words.
- Start with the part you know.

## 4. A stress interview: the interviewer is cold, interrupts or challenges every answer.

## 7. What do you do when everything is urgent at once?

**Strong answer structure:** sort by impact (safety first, then time limits) -> tell your manager what you're pushing back, and why -> ask for help if needed -> fix the system afterwards.

## More pressure questions

| Question | Quick tip |
|---|---|
| What makes you angry at work? | A mild trigger, then how you respond |
| Describe your worst day at work | Short pain, long recovery, a lesson |
| Two urgent tasks, two managers | Ask which first, confirm in writing |
| Rate yourself out of ten | A number, one reason, one gap |
| What if we set an impossible target? | Ask questions, then propose a plan |
| How do you take criticism? | Thank, clarify, act, follow up |

## Revision: four takeaways
1. Show a method, not a claim.
2. Deadlines: speak up early. Early bad news is a gift.
3. Freeze? Breathe, pause, ask for a moment.
4. Curveballs test how you think, not the final answer.

If stress at work or at home ever feels too heavy, talk to someone you trust.
