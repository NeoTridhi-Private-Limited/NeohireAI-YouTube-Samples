## 1. "What is your current C T C?"

**Structure of a strong answer:** the true fixed figure -> allowances or variable, separately -> offer to share the breakup ->
(if your pay scale is unusually low) explain it once, calmly, without apologising.

**Weak answer:** "It's okay, enough for my needs. Not much, actually." (vague, sounds like hiding something)

**Example answer:** "My current fixed pay is [amount] a year, plus [night shift / other] allowances. I can share the full breakup.
My pay follows a [government / campus] scale, so I'm expecting a market rate for this role."

## 2. "What's your expected salary?"

**Structure:** "Based on my research for [role] in [city]" -> link it to your skills -> say your two numbers -> stop talking.
The bottom of your range is a number you would truly be happy with (they will often pick it).

**Weak answer:** "Whatever is the company standard. I'm flexible. Anything is fine."

**Example answer:** "Based on my research for testers with automation skills in [city], I'm looking at a range of [low] to [high]."
(Then silence. No "sorry, it's negotiable".)

## 3. "That's a big jump from your current pay. Why do you expect so much?"

**Structure:** why your current pay is what it is -> what you have built or led since then -> price the new role, not the old salary.

**Weak answer:** "Everyone in my batch got a big hike. And my rent went up."

**Example answer:** "My current pay was set when I joined as a fresher. Since then I've built our [automation suite] and I lead
[release testing]. My range reflects this role and its market."

## 4. "Our package is part fixed, part commission (or variable). Is that okay?"

**Structure:** agree in principle -> ask how targets are set -> ask what people in the role actually earned -> ask which parts are
monthly and which are yearly (allowances such as housing and transport are often listed separately in some countries).

**Weak answer:** "Yes, yes, commission is fine. I'll sell everything."

**Example answer:** "I'm comfortable with commission. Could you share how the targets are set, and what the team earned on average
last year? And which parts of the package are monthly, and which are yearly?"

## 6. "Before we go further, just give me a number."

**Structure:** if you have researched well, give your range. If the role is still unclear, defer politely once and ask for the band.
If they insist a second time, give your researched range calmly.

**Weak answer:** panicking and quoting your last appraisal figure "plus whatever feels lucky".

**Example answer:** "I'd like to understand the responsibilities first. Could you share the budgeted band for this role?"
If they insist: "Based on my research, my range is [low] to [high]."

## 7. After a career break: "Your last salary is three years old. What are you expecting now?"

**Structure:** say you have researched current ranges -> show your skills are current -> give a range for the role today.
No apology for the break.

**Weak answer:** "Anything is okay. I've been away. I'm just grateful to get a job."

**Example answer:** "My last salary is three years old, so I've researched current ranges for this role. I've kept my skills current
with [a course / certification] this year. I'm looking at a range of [low] to [high], in line with the market."

## More salary questions (one-line tips)

| Question | Tip | Your answer |
|---|---|---|
| What's the minimum you'd accept? | Share your range, not your floor | |
| Would you join for the same C T C? | Say honestly what the move must offer you | |
| Lower fixed, higher variable? | Ask how the variable was paid last year | |
| What was your last hike? | Answer truly, then bring it back to this role | |
| Is your expectation negotiable? | Open on the full package, with reasons | |
| Why do you want more than your colleagues? | Talk about your skills, not about people | |

## Revision: four takeaways

1. Research current ranges for your city and role (sites, people, recruiters).
2. Read the C T C: know your fixed pay, your variable pay and your in hand.
3. Give a range. Its bottom should be a number that makes you happy.
4. Be truthful about your current C T C. Frame it well; never round it up.

*General interview guidance, not a guarantee of any outcome. Salary practices and laws differ by country, company and role.*
