## Before the call: your setup checklist

- [ ] Light in front of you, not behind
- [ ] Camera at eye level (a few books under the laptop)
- [ ] Quiet room, phone on silent, fan or window noise handled
- [ ] Earphones with a mic tested the day before
- [ ] Plan B: charged laptop, phone ready as a hotspot, interviewer's phone number saved
- [ ] Family told the interview time
- [ ] A few keywords written near the camera (not a script)

## 4. AI interview, timed: "Tell me about a time you handled a difficult customer / patient." (90 seconds)

**Structure:** Situation (one line), Action (what YOU did, two or three steps), Result (what changed). Finish before the timer.

**Example (a nurse):** "An elderly patient refused his medicine at night. He was scared and confused. I sat with him, explained each tablet slowly, and called his son on speaker. He took the medicine and slept. The next morning he asked for me by name."

## 6. "Do you have any questions for us?"

**Structure:** always say yes. Ask two real questions you want answered. Salary and leave are not the first question.

**Example:** "Yes. What would a good first three months look like in this role? And what do people who do well on this team have in common?"

**More questions you can ask:**

| Question | Why it helps |
|---|---|
| What does a good first three months look like? | Shows you plan to deliver |
| How is success measured in this role? | Shows you care about results |
| What training do new joiners get? | Shows you want to grow |
| What is the team working on now? | Shows real interest |
| What are the next steps, and when? | Tells you when to follow up |
| Do you enjoy working here? (asked kindly) | Their face tells you a lot |
| Avoid: anything the website already says | It shows you did not look |

## 7. The thank-you email and the follow-up

**Thank-you (same day), structure:** thank them, name one thing you enjoyed discussing, say you're still keen. Two or three lines. Check the spelling of their name. Reply in the same email thread.

**Example:** "Dear Ms Rao, thank you for your time today. I enjoyed hearing about the new reporting project. I'm very keen on the role."

**Follow-up (after about a week of silence), structure:** thank again, still interested, ask about the timeline. One more gentle note a week later at most, then keep applying elsewhere.

**Example:** "Thank you again for Tuesday's conversation. I'm still very interested. Is there an update on the timeline?"

## Body language and dress (in person and on video)

- Wait to be offered a seat. Follow the interviewer's lead on a handshake or namaste.
- Sit upright, hands relaxed. Phone in the bag.
- On video: sit a little back so shoulders and hands show; look at the lens when you speak, at their face when you listen.
- Clean, ironed, plain colours. Unsure of the dress code? Ask the recruiter.

## Four takeaways

1. Prepare the room, and keep a plan B.
2. Speak to the lens, listen to the face.
3. Answer the exact question asked, in time.
4. Ask two real questions, then send a short thank-you.

*Platforms and employers differ. Read the instructions each one gives you. Nothing here guarantees an outcome.*
