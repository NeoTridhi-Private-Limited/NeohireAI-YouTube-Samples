# Crack the HR Interview: Formal and Panel Interviews (answer sheet)

Bank and PSU interviews, MBA personal interviews, hospital and government panels, and group discussions. Write your answers
before the interview, then practise them aloud in a formal register: complete sentences, no slang, polite but not stiff.
Keep each answer to about one minute. Never bluff: panels often know the answer and ask follow-up questions.

**The pattern for formal answers**
1. Greet the panel; address members as "sir" or "ma'am", or follow how they introduce themselves.
2. Answer the question that was asked, with one real example from your own life or work.
3. For opinion questions, give both sides, a clear view, and one practical step. No invented figures.
4. If you don't know, say so calmly, offer what you do know, and say you will read up on it.
5. Look first at the person who asked, include the others as you go, and finish with the person who asked.

## Facing a panel

- Start by looking at the person who asked the question.
- Include the other members as you continue.
- Finish your answer with the person who asked.
- Greet and thank the whole panel. Don't read too much into someone writing or looking bored.

## More formal panel questions (quick tips)

| Question | One-line tip |
|---|---|
| Why should we not select you? | Name a real gap calmly, and what you're doing about it |
| What if you're posted to a remote branch? | Be truthful; show willingness and ask about the posting policy |
| Who is your role model? | Someone you really know about: the follow-up questions will test it |
| Basics of your own field | Revise them: panels test fundamentals |
| A news story you followed | One you can explain from both sides |
| Any questions for us? | One question about the role or the training |
