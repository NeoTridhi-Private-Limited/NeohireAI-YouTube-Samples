# Crack the HR Interview: What HR Really Checks, and Tell Me About Yourself

## Answer sheet

Write your own answer under each question. Keep every answer **true**: the examples are templates, not scripts to copy.
Time yourself, then say each answer aloud to a friend or a mirror.

**What the HR round checks:** fit, honesty, stability, communication, salary fit.
Every answer you give is quietly tested against these five.

---

### 1. Tell me about yourself (fresher)

**Strong answer structure** (60 to 90 seconds, then stop)
1. Present: who you are now (degree or role) and your main strength.
2. Past: one or two highlights that led here, with one real result or lesson.
3. Future: why this role is your sensible next step.

**Avoid:** family details, a list of hobbies, "hardworking and a team player" with no proof.

**Example answer**
> I'm a final-year computer engineering student in Pune, and I enjoy building small web apps. In my third year I built an
> attendance app for our department, and real students used it for a whole semester. I learned to fix bugs quickly when real
> users complain. Now I want to join a product team like yours and learn to build at scale.

### 2. Tell me about yourself (experienced)

**Strong answer structure**
1. Present: your role, years of experience, and your area.
2. Proof: one thing you are known for, with a result.
3. Future: why you are moving, and why this role.

**Avoid:** the "timeline trap" (every job in order, "then... then... then...", no point at the end). Personal details are your choice.

**Example answer**
> I'm a staff nurse with eight years of experience, the last three in intensive care. I'm known for calm handovers, and I trained
> six junior nurses on our ICU checklist. I'm moving to a private hospital because I want to work with newer equipment, and I want

### 3. Walk me through your resume

**Strong answer structure**
1. Pick a path: show the line from each job to this one.
2. For each step, one thing you grew into.
3. End at this role as the natural next step.

**Avoid:** reading the resume aloud, page by page and date by date. The interviewer already has a copy.

**Example answer**
> I started in field sales, covering small shops across Lagos. My area grew each year, and I was asked to train the new reps.
> In my current role I manage key accounts, and I plan routes and targets for a small team. So area sales manager is the natural
> next step: I'm already doing parts of it.

### 5. What do you do in your free time?

**Strong answer structure**
- Your real hobby, said simply.
- One specific detail or recent example.
- (Optional) what it shows: patience, problem solving, teamwork.

**Avoid:** a hobby you can't talk about. Expect at least one follow-up question.

**Example answer**
> I watch a lot of cooking videos, and I try one new recipe every Sunday. Last month I made dosa batter from scratch. It failed

### 6. How long do you see yourself staying with us?

**Strong answer structure**
- You want a place to grow for several years.
- What you hope to learn or take on in this team (from your research).
- You stay while you keep learning and contributing.

**Avoid:** "until I get a better offer" (too blunt) and "forever" (not believable). Give a reason, not a promise.

**Example answer**
> I'm looking for a place where I can grow for several years. From what I've read about your team, I can learn testing first,
> then automation, and then lead a small area. If I keep learning and contributing, I don't see a reason to leave.

### 7. Tell me something that isn't on your resume

**Strong answer structure**
- One true, human detail.
- Link it to a skill the job needs.

**Avoid:** "I am a very good person". Never invent a story: find the true one that fits.

**Example answer** (a returning mother)
> I ran the parents' association at my son's school. I managed volunteers and a small budget. It's not on my resume, but it kept

## Four takeaways
1. HR checks fit, honesty, stability, communication and salary fit.
2. Tell me about yourself: present, past, future. 60 to 90 seconds, then stop.
3. Every claim needs a small proof: a project or a short story.
4. Keep it true: one follow-up question finds anything you invent.

*General interview guidance, not a guarantee of any outcome. Practices differ by country, company and role.*
