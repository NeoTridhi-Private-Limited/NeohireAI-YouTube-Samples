## When an offer arrives: five calm steps

1. Say thank you, and show real interest (even if the number disappoints you).
2. Ask for the full offer in writing: fixed and variable pay, benefits, joining date.
3. Ask for a day or two to review it.
4. Compare fixed pay and in hand, not just the headline C T C.
5. Negotiate once, with reasons.

## 1. A lowball offer: "We can offer this package. Can you accept today?"

**Structure:** thank them and show interest -> ask for the full offer in writing and a day to review -> next day, counter once
with research and results -> ask about flexibility -> stop talking.

**Weak answers:** "Okay, fine, yes" (and regret it), or "That's an insult."

**Example answer:** "Thank you, I'm really excited about this role and the team. Could you send the full offer in writing?
I'd like a day to review it." Next day: "Based on my research for this role in [city] and my [results], I was expecting a range
closer to [low] to [high]. Is there flexibility on the fixed pay?"

## 2. "Do you have any other offers?"

**Structure:** only if it is true -> say you have one -> why this company is your first choice -> ask about flexibility.
You don't have to name the other company. Never invent an offer.

**Weak answer:** "Yes, a very big company offered me double. Match it, or I'll go." (a threat, and maybe a lie)

**Example answer:** "Yes, I have one other offer. Your company is my first choice because of [the product and the team].
The other offer is higher on fixed pay. Is there any flexibility to close the gap?"

## 3. "If your current company counters, will you stay?"

**Structure:** you've thought it through before resigning -> you're leaving for the role and growth, not just pay -> you're committed.

**Weak answer:** "It depends on how much they offer. Let's see."

**Example answer:** "I've thought this through before resigning. I'm leaving for the role and the growth, not just the pay.
I'm committed to joining you."

**Before you accept a counter-offer, ask yourself:** Why did I want to leave? Will money fix the role, the manager or the growth?
Will the trust be the same? Is the counter in writing? Then decide once, and keep your word.

**Declining a counter-offer from your manager:** "Thank you, I really value what you've offered. I've accepted another role,
and I've decided to go. I'd like to make the handover smooth. Let's plan it together."

## 4. "The fixed pay is already at the top of the band."

**Structure:** respect their limit -> ask for a different lever: a joining bonus, or an early review date.
Read the joining-bonus clause: many must be paid back if you leave within a set time.

**Weak answer:** "Then I'll think about it." (and three days of silence)

**Example answer:** "I understand the band. Since the fixed is fixed, could we look at a joining bonus to close the gap in the
first year? Or could we agree on a review after [six] months?"

## 5. Notice period: "We need you to join in thirty days. Can you manage?"

**Structure:** state your real notice period -> offer to ask for an early release -> ask if they support a notice buyout ->
get the answer in the offer letter. (A buyout means paying your old company for notice days you don't serve; some new
employers pay or reimburse it, some don't.)

**Weak answer:** "Yes, yes, I'll manage somehow."

**Example answer:** "My notice period is [ninety] days. I'll ask my manager for an early release. If that isn't possible,
would you support a notice buyout? I'd like to join as early as I can."

## 6. "We can't move on salary. Is there anything else?"

**Structure:** pick one or two non-salary items that matter most -> a reason for each -> ask to include them in the offer.
Options: title or level, joining date, remote or hybrid days, learning budget or certifications, first review date, relocation help.

**Weak answer:** "Oh. Okay. No, nothing. Thank you." (or a list of ten demands with no reasons)

**Example answer:** "I understand. Two things would help me most: two remote days a week while my son settles into school,
and support for a certification this year. Could we include both in the offer?"

## 7. The title: "The offer is for [a lower title]."

**Weak answer:** "Title doesn't matter. I'm just happy to join."

**Example answer:** "I've led a ward shift for two years. Could the title reflect that? If not now, could we agree on a review

## 8. "Great, you've agreed. Just confirm on the phone. The letter will come next week."

**Structure:** thank them -> confirm once you have it in writing -> check every agreed item is in the letter -> resign only after
the written offer.

**Weak answer:** "Yes, done. I'll resign today itself."

**Example answer:** "That's great news, thank you. I'm happy to confirm once I have the offer in writing."

**The letter should include:** the title and role, fixed and variable pay, joining date and location, and anything special you
agreed (bonus, remote days, buyout support). Ask for corrections, politely, before you sign.

## 9. Abroad (for example the U S): "What did you make at your last job?" / "What are your salary expectations?"

**Background:** U S offers are often described as base (yearly salary), bonus (often a target, not a promise) and equity
(often stock units that vest over years), plus benefits (health insurance, retirement match, paid leave).
Many places restrict employers from asking about salary history. **Rules differ by place: check yours.**

**Weak answer:** sharing everything, and receiving an offer a little above your old pay.

**Example answer:** "I'd rather focus on this role. Based on my research, I'm looking for a base salary in the range of
[low] to [high]."

## When to walk away, and how

Walk away when: the gap is below your floor and won't move; there is pressure ("accept today or lose it"); there is no written
offer, only promises; or there are red flags in the offer or the process (covered later in the course).

**Example:** "Thank you for your time and the offer. The gap is too large for me right now. I'd be glad to stay in touch."

## Revision: four takeaways

1. Thank them, ask for time, and get the offer in writing.
2. Counter once, with research and value. Not with anger.
3. Never invent an offer, and never lie about your pay.
4. Know your floor. If you must walk away, walk away politely.

*General interview guidance, not a guarantee of any outcome. Salary practices, notice periods and laws differ by country,
company and role.*
